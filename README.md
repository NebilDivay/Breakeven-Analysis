# Break-Even & Cost Optimization Analysis

## Project Overview

This project evaluates the cost structure and break-even performance of a food business to identify opportunities to reduce the **variable cost per unit from ₹95 to ₹90**.

The analysis focuses on understanding **fixed costs, variable costs, contribution margin, unit economics, and break-even volume**. Operational costs were reviewed at an item level to identify opportunities for cost reduction without increasing the selling price.

The final Excel model compares the **previous cost of ₹95 per piece with the revised cost of ₹90 per piece** and evaluates how the ₹5 reduction impacts contribution margin and break-even requirements.

---

## Business Problem

The existing cost of producing and packaging one saleable piece was approximately:

**₹95 per piece**

The objective was to reduce this to:

**₹90 per piece**

### Target Cost Saving

₹95 - ₹90 = **₹5 per piece**

### Percentage Cost Reduction

(₹5 / ₹95) × 100 = **5.26%**

Rather than applying an arbitrary ₹5 reduction, the analysis evaluates individual operating costs and identifies specific sourcing and packaging changes that can contribute to the cost reduction.

---

## Project Objectives

The main objectives of the project were to:

- Analyze the existing cost structure
- Separate **fixed and variable costs**
- Calculate variable expenses on a **per-piece basis**
- Analyze five days of operating expenses
- Allocate costs based on actual sales volume
- Identify unnecessary packaging and ingredient costs
- Compare the **previous ₹95 cost with the revised ₹90 cost**
- Calculate contribution margin before and after optimization
- Calculate monthly and daily break-even volume
- Measure the financial impact of the proposed cost-saving initiatives
- Develop a dynamic Excel model where calculations update when assumptions change

216 pieces/day × 5 days = **1080 pieces**

54 chickens/day × 5 days = **270 chickens**

The **1080 pieces sold during the five-day period** are used as the allocation basis for calculating the variable cost per piece.

## Variable Cost Analysis

Variable costs are expenses that increase or decrease depending on production and sales volume.

The analysis includes costs such as:

- Chicken
- Mayonnaise
- Vegetables and groceries
- Kubus
- Ketchup
- Charcoal
- Apple cider/vinegar
- Water
- Salad packing
- Mayo packing
- Silver pouches
- Carry bags
- Garbage bags
- Brown covers
- Packaging for marinated chicken

For expenses recorded as a total for the five-day period, the cost per piece is calculated as:

### Formula

**Cost per Piece = Five-Day Expense / Total Pieces Sold**

Since 1080 pieces were sold:

**Cost per Piece = Five-Day Expense / 1080**

### Example: Mayonnaise

Five-day mayonnaise cost = **₹1,080**

₹4,080 / 1080 = **₹3.78 per piece**

The same methodology is used for other expenses incurred during the five-day operating period.

For items where the cost is already known on a per-piece basis:

**Five-Day Cost = Cost per Piece × 1080**

This creates a consistent basis for analyzing all variable expenses.

# Cost Optimization Initiatives

## 1. Mayonnaise Sourcing

### Previous Practice

Mayonnaise was prepared in-house.

However, rising cooking oil prices increased the cost of producing homemade mayonnaise.

### Revised Practice

A commercially available mayonnaise with a similar taste was identified at a lower cost.

### Benefits

- Reduces ingredient costs
- Reduces exposure to increases in cooking oil prices
- Simplifies food preparation
- Provides more predictable costs
- Maintains product consistency

---

## 2. Ketchup Packaging

### Previous Practice

Ketchup was portioned in the kitchen and packed into small ziplock bags.

Once the ketchup was packed, the business had already incurred:

- The cost of ketchup
- The cost of the ziplock bag
- The risk of wasting the packed ketchup if the customer did not want it

### Revised Practice

Individual ketchup sachets are used instead.

Ketchup is provided **only when requested by the customer**.

### Benefits

- Reduces ketchup wastage
- Eliminates unnecessary ziplock packaging
- Reduces preparation requirements
- Reduces packaging costs
- Creates a demand-based distribution process

---

## 3. Aluminium Foil Replacement

### Previous Practice

Aluminium foil was used for food packaging.

The packaging occasionally caused leakage, requiring an additional plastic cover.

This meant the business incurred both:

1. Aluminium foil cost
2. Additional plastic packaging cost

### Revised Practice

The aluminium foil packaging was replaced with a **silver pouch**.

### Benefits

- Reduces leakage
- Reduces the need for secondary plastic packaging
- Reduces overall packaging costs
- Improves packaging consistency

---

## 4. Brown Bag Packaging

A lower-cost brown bag was introduced as an alternative to the existing outer packaging.

### Benefits

- Lower packaging cost
- Reduced dependence on plastic packaging
- More environmentally friendly packaging
- Contributes to the overall ₹5 cost-saving target

**₹90 per piece**

### Saving per Piece

₹95 - ₹90 = **₹5**

# Contribution Margin Analysis

The selling price used in the analysis is:

**₹120 per piece**

Contribution margin represents the amount remaining after variable costs are deducted from the selling price.

### Formula

**Contribution Margin = Selling Price - Variable Cost**

## Before Cost Optimization

₹120 - ₹95 = **₹25 per piece**

## After Cost Optimization

₹120 - ₹90 = **₹30 per piece**

### Improvement in Contribution Margin

₹30 - ₹25 = **₹5 per piece**

### Percentage Improvement

(₹5 / ₹25) × 100 = **20%**

The operational cost reduction therefore increases the contribution generated by each piece by **20%**.

---

# Fixed Cost Analysis

The monthly fixed-cost base used in the model is:

**₹246,564 per month**

Fixed costs are expenses that generally remain payable regardless of short-term production or sales volume.

The fixed costs are combined with the contribution margin to determine the number of pieces that must be sold to reach break-even.

---

# Break-Even Analysis

Break-even occurs when:

**Total Revenue = Total Cost**

At the break-even point, the business generates neither a profit nor a loss.

### Formula

**Break-Even Units = Fixed Costs / Contribution Margin per Unit**

---

## Break-Even Before Cost Reduction

### Inputs

- Selling Price = ₹120
- Variable Cost = ₹95
- Contribution Margin = ₹25
- Fixed Costs = ₹246,564

### Calculation

₹246,564 / ₹25 = **9,862.56 pieces**

Therefore, approximately **9,863 pieces per month** must be sold to reach break-even under the previous cost structure.

---

## Break-Even After Cost Reduction

### Inputs

- Selling Price = ₹120
- Variable Cost = ₹90
- Contribution Margin = ₹30
- Fixed Costs = ₹246,564

### Calculation

₹246,564 / ₹30 = **8,218.80 pieces**

Therefore, approximately **8,219 pieces per month** must be sold to reach break-even under the revised cost structure.

---

# Impact on Break-Even Volume

### Reduction in Required Monthly Sales Volume

9,862.56 - 8,218.80 = **1,643.76 pieces**

Approximately **1,644 fewer pieces per month** need to be sold to reach break-even.

### Percentage Reduction in Break-Even Volume

(1,643.76 / 9,862.56) × 100 = **16.67%**

The cost optimization therefore reduces the required break-even volume by approximately **16.67%**.

---

# Daily Break-Even Requirement

Assuming **30 operating days per month**:

## Before Optimization

9,862.56 / 30 = **328.75 pieces per day**

Approximately **329 pieces per day**

## After Optimization

8,218.80 / 30 = **273.96 pieces per day**

Approximately **274 pieces per day**

### Reduction

329 - 274 = **55 fewer pieces per day**

The cost optimization reduces the daily sales requirement needed to reach break-even by approximately **55 pieces**.

---

# Current Sales vs Break-Even

Current sales are approximately:

**216 pieces per day**

Assuming 30 operating days:

216 × 30 = **6,480 pieces per month**

The Excel model compares the current sales volume against the required break-even volume.

This helps identify the gap between **current operating performance and the sales level required to fully cover the existing fixed-cost structure**.
